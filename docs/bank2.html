Why this bank sits where it does

Opens with a 12-entry JMP table at $8000-$8023. Nothing else in this bank has been traced.

An address on this page does not identify a byte. $8000-$BFFF is the switchable window, so every address below exists in seven banks at once and only bank 2 has these particular bytes at it. Any address taken from a debugger, a wiki or another disassembly has to carry a bank with it before it means anything — tools/whereis.py --bytes settles which bank really holds a given sequence.

Font upload records pointer_table confirmed

bank2.tbl_80EC · file 0x080FC-0x0811A · 31 bytes · PRG bank 2 · CPU $80EC-$810A

Six 5-byte records, [glyph count][dest lo][dest hi][src lo][src hi], terminated by the $FF at $810A. Driven by $80C5, which reads them through $80EC,Y and steps Y by 5 at $80E3, and called in a loop from $80BB - this bank's entry slot 0, which the title-screen path far-calls from bank 0 $8915. The records: 10 glyphs to $1300, 27 to $1400, and one each to $15B0, $15C0, $15D0 and $15E0. THOSE DESTINATIONS ARE THE CHARACTER ENCODING, stated by the ROM rather than inferred from context: $1300 is pattern table 1 tile $30, so ten glyphs there are the digits at ASCII $30-$39; $1400 is tile $40, so 27 glyphs are $40-$5A, an @ followed by A-Z. That is the first evidence for the encoding in this project that does not come from reading English out of a payload. The four single-glyph records are why different screens disagree about punctuation: $5B, $5C, $5D and $5E are each patched individually, so what sits at those tile indices is whatever the last record set put there.

bank2.bank_id_BFFF · file 0x0C00F-0x0C00F · 1 bytes · PRG bank 2 · CPU $BFFF-$BFFF

One byte at $BFFF, the last byte of PRG bank 2, holding the bank's own number. Banks 0 to 6 each carry one; bank 7 does not, because bank 7's last byte is at $FFFF and belongs to the IRQ vector. These seven bytes are the ROM half of a boot-time mapper self-test. $FF23-$FF43, reached from the reset handler, is a loop over Y = 6 down to 0: TYA / JSR $D705 selects the bank in Y, CPY $BFFF then compares Y against the byte this region holds, and BEQ $FF41 falls through to DEY only when the two agree. If they do not, LDA $FF3A,Y fetches a per-bank code, stores it at $0180, calls $DED2 and then spins forever at JMP $FF37. So the game refuses to start unless every one of the seven switchable banks can be selected and answers with its own number - which is the check a UxROM board needs, because a bus conflict on the latch fails silently and leaves the wrong bank mapped. Reading the byte at $BFFF works no matter which bank is live precisely because every bank has one at the same address and they all differ.
