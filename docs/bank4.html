Why this bank sits where it does

Opens with a single JMP at $8000-$8002 and then falls into what decodes as code at $8003. The one-entry table is the odd one out among the eight banks and has not been explained.

An address on this page does not identify a byte. $8000-$BFFF is the switchable window, so every address below exists in seven banks at once and only bank 4 has these particular bytes at it. Any address taken from a debugger, a wiki or another disassembly has to carry a bank with it before it means anything — tools/whereis.py --bytes settles which bank really holds a given sequence.

bank4.tbl_8013 · file 0x10023-0x10054 · 50 bytes · PRG bank 4 · CPU $8013-$8044

25 little-endian pointers, $8013-$8044, each naming a block in this bank. Read by this bank's only entry point: $8000 jumps to $8003, which does CLC / ADC #$02 / ASL A / TAY and then LDA $8013,Y / STA $18 and LDA $8014,Y / STA $19, returning the block address in $18/$19. So entry n sits at $8017 + 2n and the index arrives in A. THE INDEX IS THE AREA NUMBER: bank 7 $EF9B switches to bank 4, $EF9E does LDA $51 and $EFA0 calls $8000. $51 is bounded by CPY #$15 at $F2E2, which is 21 - and 21 areas is what the game has. The table's own 25 entries reach two slots below the index-zero entry and two above index 21, and what those four are for is NOT established. The upper bound on the table is structural: entry 2 points at $8045, the byte immediately after the table, so the table cannot extend past $8044. This is how bank 4 is reached at all - the bank sat at 19 traced bytes of 16,384 because its entry point returns a pointer instead of running code, and nothing static followed the pointer.

bank4.bank_id_BFFF · file 0x1400F-0x1400F · 1 bytes · PRG bank 4 · CPU $BFFF-$BFFF

One byte at $BFFF, the last byte of PRG bank 4, holding the bank's own number. Banks 0 to 6 each carry one; bank 7 does not, because bank 7's last byte is at $FFFF and belongs to the IRQ vector. These seven bytes are the ROM half of a boot-time mapper self-test. $FF23-$FF43, reached from the reset handler, is a loop over Y = 6 down to 0: TYA / JSR $D705 selects the bank in Y, CPY $BFFF then compares Y against the byte this region holds, and BEQ $FF41 falls through to DEY only when the two agree. If they do not, LDA $FF3A,Y fetches a per-bank code, stores it at $0180, calls $DED2 and then spins forever at JMP $FF37. So the game refuses to start unless every one of the seven switchable banks can be selected and answers with its own number - which is the check a UxROM board needs, because a bus conflict on the latch fails silently and leaves the wrong bank mapped. Reading the byte at $BFFF works no matter which bank is live precisely because every bank has one at the same address and they all differ.
